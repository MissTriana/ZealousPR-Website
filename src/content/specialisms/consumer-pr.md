Consumer PR has to earn attention from people who didn't ask for it — and increasingly, it has to earn a place in the answers AI engines give when those same people go looking for advice, recommendations, or reassurance. "Is this a good mortgage rate," "should I buy in this development," "is this brand worth trusting" — these are consumer questions being asked of AI as often as they're typed into a search bar.

That means consumer PR now has two audiences at once: the person, and the AI engine standing between them and the information they're after. Getting cited accurately by the second is what earns trust with the first.

## Sectors this spans

Consumer-facing work has run deepest in **personal finance** — where accuracy and trust are non-negotiable — and the resident- and homebuyer-facing side of **property & placemaking**, where community engagement has to hold up to real local scrutiny. It also draws on broader consumer brand experience, including automotive.

## What this looks like in practice

- Consumer media relations and press office, mapped to what actually matters to your audience
- Community engagement communications that hold up under genuine local scrutiny
- Content built to be accurate and citable — critical in sectors like personal finance where AI-generated answers carry real consequences
- Social media and owned content that speaks to people directly, not just at them
